Making it real

What it takes to put this card in a wallet

This page is a product design. Nothing here can be spent. A real card needs a licensed bank behind it, and the route there has a fixed order.

  1. Pick the model

    Co-brand with an existing issuer, or run your own programme through a sponsor bank that holds the licence and the credit risk rules.

  2. Choose an issuer processor

    Platforms such as Stripe Issuing, Marqeta or Lithic create the card numbers, authorise each charge and expose the controls shown above as APIs.

  3. Get network approval

    The card network assigns a number range, reviews the artwork and certifies the metal card and chip.

  4. Build compliance and underwriting

    Identity checks, anti-money-laundering monitoring, credit decisions, disclosures and dispute handling. In the US this means the CARD Act and Regulation Z.

  5. Fund the credit line

    Someone lends the money between purchase and payment. That is a bank balance sheet or a debt facility.

  6. Make the rewards add up

    Fees, partners and interest have to cover what the points cost. See the gap below.

The gap this design has to close

5× at 1.5¢ returns 7.5% of travel spend to the cardholder. A US credit card earns its issuer roughly 2% of each purchase from the merchant. The difference is paid for by the annual fee, by airline and hotel partners buying points, and by cardholders who carry a balance. That is why the fee is $695 and why cards this generous are rare.

Tessera is an invented name used for this concept. Earn rates, credits, fee and benefits are design targets, not an offer of credit.
